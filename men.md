## Why men need a different analysis framework

Men's facial attractiveness research consistently shows that different dimensions drive scores for men vs. women. For men, structural features dominate: jawline definition, cheekbone prominence, brow ridge strength, facial symmetry, and the overall sense of structural sharpness. YanSheng's male analysis is calibrated to these dimensions — not a gender-neutral average, and not Western standards that don't apply to Asian bone structure.

## What YanSheng analyzes for men

### Jawline definition — the single most important male feature
In East Asian male aesthetics, jawline clarity is the dominant attractiveness signal. YanSheng measures jaw angle sharpness, jaw width relative to cheekbone width, mandible definition, and chin projection and shape. The ideal male jaw in East Asian standards is well-defined, with clear angularity — not necessarily wide, but visually present.

**Why it matters:** A soft, undefined jaw is the most common reason an otherwise good-looking man photographs poorly or gets underestimated in person. It is also the feature with the highest improvement potential through targeted facial exercises, body fat reduction, and styling choices.

**What the score tells you:** Whether your jaw is genuinely undefined (structural), whether it's obscured by fat distribution (addressable), or whether it's actually well-defined but positioned in a way that photographs poorly (fixable with angle awareness).

### Bone structure and facial sharpness
Beyond the jaw, YanSheng evaluates overall skeletal sharpness: cheekbone projection and position, temple width, brow ridge definition, and the overall sense of three-dimensionality in the face. Asian male faces vary enormously in bone prominence — from very flat and smooth to highly defined — and the scoring accounts for this range rather than penalizing features that fall outside a Western ideal.

**Why it matters:** Bone structure is the primary determinant of how a man photographs, how he reads in a room, and how his face changes with age. Understanding your structural baseline tells you what you're working with and where styling choices can add the most visual impact.

### Face shape and proportion
YanSheng classifies male face shape (oval, square, rectangular, round, diamond, triangular) and evaluates overall proportion: face length to width ratio, upper-to-lower face balance, and mid-face length. For men, a longer face reads as more mature and dominant in East Asian culture. A wider face can read as strong but risks appearing heavy if not balanced correctly.

**Why it matters:** Men's hairstyle choices should be based on face shape, not trend. The wrong haircut on the wrong face shape creates visual imbalance that works against everything else.

### Eye shape and intensity
Men's eye aesthetics prioritize depth, intensity, and definition over size. YanSheng evaluates eye shape (almond, round, deep-set, prominent, hooded), eye spacing, brow-to-eye distance, and the overall sense of visual impact the eye area creates. For men, hooded eyes can create a naturally intense expression — or they can make the face look tired. The distinction matters.

**Why it matters:** Eye grooming (brow shaping, under-eye care, eyelid definition) is one of the highest-leverage improvements available to men, but most men apply generic advice that doesn't account for their specific eye shape.

### Brow shape and position
The brow is the frame of the male face. YanSheng measures brow thickness, arch shape (flat, medium, high), brow-to-eye distance, and brow length. In East Asian male aesthetics, thicker, straighter brows project strength. Arched brows can add intensity. Overly thin or poorly shaped brows are one of the most common grooming mistakes men make.

**Why it matters:** Brow grooming is the single highest-ROI grooming investment for most men. A properly shaped brow changes the entire read of a face. YanSheng tells you exactly what shape works for your face, not a generic template.

### Nose structure and prominence
Male nose aesthetics favor a stronger, more prominent nose than female standards. YanSheng evaluates nose bridge height (relative to face depth, not absolute), nose width, tip definition, and dorsal shape. A wider nose on a man with a strong jaw can look powerful. The same nose on a man with a soft jaw looks unbalanced. Context matters.

**Why it matters:** Many Asian men have internalized insecurity about nose bridge height based on Western comparisons. YanSheng evaluates your nose in the context of East Asian proportion standards and your specific face structure.

### Skin tone and complexion
YanSheng classifies male skin undertone (warm, cool, neutral) and evaluates skin clarity as a component of the overall score. For men, skin clarity is a strong attractiveness signal — clear skin on a man with average bone structure consistently outscores textured skin on a man with good bone structure in research.

**Why it matters:** Skincare is the highest-leverage investment most men are not making. YanSheng tells you your undertone, which informs which skincare ingredients and product types suit your complexion, and which clothing colors enhance your natural coloring.

### Facial symmetry
YanSheng evaluates left-right facial symmetry across multiple landmarks. Symmetry is one of the most robust predictors of perceived attractiveness across cultures. Importantly, most asymmetry is minor and imperceptible in normal social interaction — what matters is whether asymmetry is affecting your score significantly or negligibly.

**Why it matters:** Many men worry about asymmetry they've noticed in photos. YanSheng tells you whether your asymmetry is actually at a level that affects your score or whether it's within normal human variation.

## Improvement directions for men

YanSheng identifies the single highest-impact non-medical improvement direction for your specific face. Examples:

**For men with soft jawlines:** Targeted facial exercises that strengthen masseter and digastric muscles. Hairstyle choices that add visual definition to the lower face. Styling choices that draw attention to stronger upper-face features.

**For men with round faces:** Hairstyles that add height and vertical emphasis. Clothing neckline choices that elongate the perceived face shape. Beard styling (if applicable) that creates visual jaw definition.

**For men with flat bone structure:** Lighting and photography angle awareness. Grooming choices that add three-dimensionality. Color contrast strategies in clothing that make features read more prominently.

**For men with strong bone structure but skin issues:** Targeted skincare protocol. Understanding which ingredients address their specific skin texture. Lifestyle factors affecting skin clarity.

**For men with asymmetry:** Camera angle awareness. Hairstyle choices that visually correct the more prominent asymmetry. Grooming adjustments that balance the face.
